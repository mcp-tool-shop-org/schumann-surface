Shell

One-station inversion: the pattern is the associated-Legendre magnetic standing wave of each measured mode, centred on the chimneys and scaled by this station's amplitude. Yellow fans are the measured magnetic polarisation (two opposite lobes: 180° ambiguity); their width is 1 − coherence. Not a global map. Modes 4 and up are not in the data.

Time

Default 6×: one day is a short step against the frequency × amplitude plane, so the diurnal helix reads as a coil. 1× is the raw month-long streak.

What to look for

Two opposite yellow fans from Sierra Nevada — the axial polarisation is 180° ambiguous. October fans should look fat (median mode-1 coh ≈ 0.18), not a needle; a rare high-coh column narrows them. Scrub to ~14 UTC: Africa (green) should sit in a fan. ~20 UTC: the Americas (blue). October chimney dots sit south of the equator (SON); June sits north (JJA). Pin tooltip prints az / coh. The globe still says one-station inversion.

What to look for

Grey tube is the month-mean diurnal coil (amplitude from diurnal). Time is compressed so one day is a short step; use the slider if you want the raw streak. The coloured path is the data (Kp as colour). The yellow bead with the halo is the flagged storm. A path that leaves the coil is a departure, not a fourth mode.
